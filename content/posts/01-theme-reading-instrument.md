The word “theme” often suggests a surface: colors, type, spacing, and a few familiar page shapes. That description is incomplete. A theme also decides what remains visible while someone is reading, what counts as an arrival, and where the archive waits when the article ends.

## The page has a memory

An article rarely stands alone. It belongs to an Issue, a Section, an author, and a set of Topics. When those relationships disappear from the page, the reader has to reconstruct them from scraps of interface. Docszine keeps the relationships nearby without letting them crowd the text.

The left rail is an index of the Issue. The central column is the reading surface. The table of contents is a compact answer to a simple question: how long is this document, and where am I in it?

## Navigation is part of the prose

A good next link does not interrupt an article. It gives the article a horizon. That is why the end of a Docszine piece points back to the Issue and outward to related Sections and Topics.

[docszine_context title="Design context"]This site uses a Classic Theme because the reading model should be expressed through ordinary WordPress templates and content, not hidden inside a page-builder canvas.[/docszine_context]

## The smallest complete system

The ambition is not to create every possible editorial control. It is to create the smallest system that can name an Issue, order its articles, explain a section, and let the reader move on. Everything else should earn its place.
